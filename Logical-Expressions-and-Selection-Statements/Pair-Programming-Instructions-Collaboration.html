Pair Programming -- Collaboration

Why Pair Programming?

It has been used successfully by companies like Facebook, Stripe, Airbnb, Spotify, and Google for tasks such as onboarding and critical systems development.  Advantages include the following.

  • Better Code Quality and Consistency:  Real-time reviews reduce bugs and maintain uniform standards.
  • Collaboration and Team Cohesion:  Encourages communication, trust, and knowledge sharing.
  • Faster Problem-Solving: Two minds tackle challenges efficiently.
  • Accelerated Learning and Onboarding:  Boosts skill development for juniors and simplifies training.
  • Employee Growth and Engagement:  Provides mentorship and fosters confidence.

Rules

  • Respect each other’s ideas.
  • Clearly communicate your thought process.
  • Encourage your partner and maintain a supportive attitude.

Choose Partner

Classroom

Students who are able to come to class, can choose a partner during class with whom to complete this task.  Open one of the Colab notebooks (see links below).  If it already has student names at the top, choose another Colab notebook.  When you find one with no names, add your names to the top.  Follow the steps in the notebook to complete it.

Online

Students can complete this task online with a partner, either synchronously or asynchronously.  You may use this Discord server to communicate, if you like.  Please set your nickname to the actual (nick)name you're using in this class.  Unrecognized names will be removed.

Synchronous

Identify someone with whom you can schedule a meeting time, and complete the task together.  You may already know someone in the class with whom you can meet.  Alternatively, you could look through the results from the Student Profile Survey (under Course Resources on the course home page) to find another online student who likes to coordinate synchronously and plans to work on this class at the same time you do.  Send them an email to see if they'd like to meet and work on this with you. 

When you meet, open one of the Colab notebooks (see links below).  If it already has student names at the top, choose another Colab notebook.  When you find one with no names, add your names to the top.  Follow the steps in the notebook to complete it.

Asynchronous

If you wish to work asynchronously, open one of the Colab notebooks (see links below).  If it already has two student names at the top, choose another Colab notebook.  Look for one that has only one student name at the top.  Add your name as the second name.  If no Colab notebooks have just one name, find one with no names, and put your name at the top.  Follow the steps in the notebook to complete it.

If you're the first person to start on the notebook, return to the notebook after a peer has completed their portion, review their code by completing the Code Review section for their code, and make your overall observations about the experience at the bottom of the notebook.  If no one else has completed the other programming prompt by the day this is due, check out some of the other notebooks where code reviews have been done, and make your observations based on the programming prompt you completed and the other code reviews you read.
